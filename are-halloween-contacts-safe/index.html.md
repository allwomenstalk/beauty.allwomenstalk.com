# Are Halloween Contacts Safe? What to Know Before Wearing Colored Lenses

Halloween contacts are not risk-free costume accessories. Before wearing them, get an eye examination, professional fitting and prescription—even if you see perfectly without glasses. Proper care reduces risk; it does not eliminate it. If that preparation has not happened, build the costume around makeup instead.

## Why colored lenses need an eye professional

The [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/contact-lenses/about/about-decorative-contact-lenses.html) classifies decorative contacts as medical devices. That includes costume, theatrical, circle and colored lenses without vision correction. An attractive color tells you nothing about whether a lens fits your eye.

Poorly fitted lenses can damage the cornea, the clear front surface of the eye. CDC guidance describes scratches, ulcers, infection, scarring and possible blindness. [Johns Hopkins optometrist Meng Meng Xu](https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/health/expert-qa/halloween-and-eye-safety-what-you-need-to-know) also warns about allergic reactions and vision loss. A single party is not an exemption from the risks.

## Do you need a prescription with perfect vision?

In the United States, yes. The [FDA says selling decorative contacts without a prescription is illegal](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/halloween-safety-tips-costumes-candy-and-colored-contact-lenses). It also advises against making Halloween your first experience of contact-lens wear.

Rules elsewhere should be described separately:

Country
What the local eye-health body says

United Kingdom
The College of Optometrists says zero-powered colored lenses cannot legally be sold without supervision by a registered optometrist, dispensing optician or doctor.

Canada
Canadian eye-care associations say cosmetic contacts are Class II medical devices that require Health Canada licensing; fitting and care instructions are still necessary.

See the [College of Optometrists' advice](https://www.college-optometrists.org/news/2026/october/wearing-novelty-contact-lenses-this-halloween) and the [Canadian joint statement](https://www.cos-sco.ca/canadas-eye-care-professionals-warn-of-cosmetic-contact-lens-risks-ahead-of-halloween/). A US regulatory label does not substitute for Canadian licensing.

## Where should you get them?

Start with the practice that examined and fitted you. If buying elsewhere, use a reputable supplier that verifies the prescription and supplies the prescribed lenses. Avoid treating a costume-store display or an unverified website as evidence of safety. Ask your eye professional to teach you insertion, removal and the exact care routine before the event.

The College also warns that novelty designs can interfere with vision if the opening does not align with your pupil. **Do not drive in novelty lenses; arrange another way home.**

## A checklist for people who have been fitted

These points reflect CDC and eye-care-body guidance; your clinician's instructions for your particular lenses come first.

- Wash and thoroughly dry your hands before handling lenses.

- Use the recommended contact-lens cleaning and disinfecting solution. Never use tap water or saliva, and never top up old solution.

- Never share lenses, even for a quick costume photo.

- Do not sleep in cosmetic lenses.

- Follow the wear, replacement and expiry instructions. Do not simply put a dropped lens back in your eye.

- Bring your glasses and the supplies your eye professional recommends so you can remove the lenses when necessary.

## Red flags: remove the lenses and get help

**Eye pain, redness, blurred vision, light sensitivity or persistent discharge are reasons to remove the lenses and promptly contact an eye-care professional. Do not continue wearing them or wait for the party to end.** The Canadian associations and Johns Hopkins stress the importance of responding to symptoms. Do not try to treat a suspected infection with a costume-kit remedy.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are Halloween contacts safe for one night?

One night still carries risk. An examination, fitting, prescription and correct care are essential precautions.

### Do non-prescription-strength lenses need a prescription?

In the US, yes—even lenses that only change appearance require one.

### What can unprescribed lenses do to your eyes?

Poor fit and improper care can lead to corneal injury and infection, sometimes with permanent vision damage.

### Can I share a pair with a friend?

No. Never share contact lenses.

### What if my eye hurts after wearing them?

Remove them and promptly contact an eye-care professional, especially with redness, discharge, light sensitivity or changed vision.
